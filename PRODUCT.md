# Ato's Art

A marketplace for original artwork by named African artists, sold directly to collectors and first-time buyers across the continent and the diaspora.

## Register

Hybrid. The marketing surfaces (homepage, artwork detail, artist profile) are **brand**: the design IS the product. The transactional surfaces (cart, checkout, buyer orders, artist dashboard, admin moderation) are **product**: the design SERVES the product.

When in doubt about a specific surface, the rule is: anything a non-logged-in browser sees → brand. Anything a logged-in artist or admin sees → product. The buyer order pages sit on the product side; they are utility, not seduction.

## Users

Two primary audiences. Both must be served on every public surface; neither can be designed past the other.

**1. Diaspora collectors.** Africans living abroad and global collectors of African contemporary art. They browse on laptops, often in evenings or weekends, sometimes mid-research. They have higher price tolerance (GHS 1,500 to GHS 50,000+ per piece) but they need provenance, the artist's story, and a sense that what they are buying is real. They have probably already considered Saatchi Art, Artsy, and 1-54. They are skeptical of African platforms that look unprofessional. The site has to clear that bar in the first three seconds.

**2. Local Ghanaian and Nigerian buyers.** Domestic buyers in West Africa, often on phones, often on uneven mobile connections. They are price-conscious (GHS 200 to GHS 5,000 typical range) but they care about authenticity and the artist as much as the diaspora does. Trust signals (verified artist, clear pricing, real photos) carry more weight than gallery prestige. They will share artwork links with WhatsApp friends; the link preview matters.

A secondary tier exists (interior designers sourcing for hospitality, curious browsers who don't yet own original art), but no design choice should be made for them at the expense of the two primaries.

## Brand

Ato's Art is a confident, warm gallery. It is not a craft fair, not an auction house, not a Western white-cube space transplanted onto African work. It treats every artist as a named author, not a vendor. It treats every buyer as someone capable of looking, not someone who needs to be sold to.

The aesthetic is **warm African editorial**: earth tones (terracotta, ochre, moss), serif headlines with character, generous negative space, art as the hero. The visual language draws from Aké Festival print posters, Kwame Brathwaite's archive, ChimurengaChronic, and Pan-African art quarterlies, far more than from Western marketplace UI.

Core identity rules:

- The site says nothing about being "African" through pattern overlays, kente borders, or mask icons. The Africanness is in the work, the artists, and the warmth of the surfaces. Decorative tribal pastiche is rejected.
- Art images are never cropped, never overlaid with text, never given filter treatment. They appear at full integrity. The interface yields to the work.
- Artists are named, photographed, and given biographical depth. The platform never refers to "sellers" or "vendors."
- Pricing is shown in full, in local currency, with no "request price" friction.

## Tone

Direct, unhurried, confident. We assume the reader is intelligent. We don't explain that art is meaningful. We don't use the word "elevate." We don't promise "seamless" anything.

Examples of voice:

- Headlines: "Original African art, by named artists." Not: "Discover authentic African artistry."
- Empty states: "No works here yet." Not: "Looks like there's nothing to see! Try adjusting your filters."
- Errors: "Couldn't reach the server. Try again in a moment." Not: "Oops! Something went wrong."
- CTAs: "View work", "Place order", "Contact the artist." Not: "Get started", "Learn more", "Discover now."

Copy is shaped by Edidiong Okhoyibo, Yaa Gyasi, Teju Cole, more than by any SaaS handbook. Short paragraphs, real verbs, no marketing hedge.

## Anti-references

What this site must not look like:

- **AI-Vercel SaaS slop.** Pure black or pure white backgrounds, neon purple gradients, hero metrics ("10,000+ happy users"), identical 3-card feature grids with icon-heading-paragraph, glass-morphism panels, gradient text. This is the absolute number-one trap and every choice rejects it.
- **Etsy / craft-fair.** Cluttered grids, banner deals, exclamation-point copy, beige indecision.
- **Sotheby's stuffy.** Gilded serif overload, "price on request," intimidating density.
- **Tribal pastiche.** Kente borders, mud-cloth backgrounds, mask icons. Reductive and dated.

When asked to add personality or warmth, the move is never "add a pattern." It is always "increase the editorial confidence" (a longer caption, a better photograph, a quieter detail).

## Strategic principles

In priority order, these break ties when two design choices conflict:

1. **The work comes first.** Every layout, color, and motion choice yields to the artwork. If a design decision makes the art look smaller, slower, or less primary, it loses.
2. **Trust in the first three seconds.** A diaspora collector who has never heard of us must, on first sight of the homepage, feel that this is a real platform with real artists and real prices. No hedging, no "we are excited to announce," no fake metrics.
3. **Mobile parity, not mobile graveyard.** Local West-African buyers on small phones see exactly the same level of care as the desktop diaspora user. The mobile experience is not a stripped fallback; it is its own composition.
4. **Honest commerce.** Every price is fully shown. Every shipping caveat is in plain language. Every "the artist will contact you" message says exactly what will happen and roughly when. Buyers in markets where online payment is fragile need the platform to be honest about that, not pretend to be Stripe.
5. **The artist's name is always there.** No artwork appears without its artist's name attached. No order email goes out without the artist's name in it. The platform is the substrate; the artists are the product.
